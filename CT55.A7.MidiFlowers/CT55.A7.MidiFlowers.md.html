The bunching — what caused it and the fix

Measured in A3 (landed flowers after 8 s): with Swirl flowers on and standing still, up to 54 % of the flowers lay within 1 m of the back wall; travelling, ~17 %. Three causes: 1. The vortex travels with the emitter, and on its trailing side its flow pointed at the back wall, so every flower left behind was swept there. A4: while travelling the vortex turns the other way (its trailing flow leads out into the room) and fades out behind the emitter; standing still it eases back (W.vsense). 2. The air blew through the wall. A4 uses the method of images — a mirror vortex behind the wall (opposite turn, same inflow), so next to the wall the air runs along it, never into it; the turbulence's flow into the wall fades out within 1.5 m and a slight draught leads away from it. 3. Flowers were held only 6 cm from the wall face, so their petals went into it ("under the wall"). A4: the whole flower stays in front (wallZ + flower radius), an air cushion slows flowers heading at the wall, they bounce off it, the gush throws less toward the wall, and the emitter's line is further out (3.7–5.2 m). Result (test, every scene, opening state): < 5 % within 1 m of the wall, none in or under it (measured 0–2 %).

The user's request (CT55.A1)

This is a browser based, WebGL, three.js application. It operates in real time, but there is a button to start video recording. See all the problems of video recording from CT54. It should use the Midi device control from CT45.

On midi: a button to reset; a button to go forward (to the right, horizontally); a button which when hit increases the speed of going forward; a button which when hit reduces the speed; a button which emits flowers from the emitter, which is travelling to the right; a button which swirls the emitter; a button which starts the flowers swirling.

The emitted flowers are colorful, bright, pastel, do soft shadows onto the floor. We are looking down to the floor. Buttons on the lower part of the screen to use when midi is not there. Can adjust camera point of view with the mouse. A button to increase flower emission rate; a button to decrease flower emission rate. A pulse emitter which when started emits the flowers on a pulse (to be set). A button to start recording video with audio, and stop it when hit again.

How to run

  1. Double-click index.html → Chrome or Edge (Web MIDI and the movie encoder need a Chromium browser; Firefox can view but not use MIDI or record).
  2. Chrome asks to allow MIDI ("control and reprogram MIDI devices" = sysex, for full Launchpad colours). Allow it. If you said no, the MIDI button (bottom right) → Connect MIDI devices asks again.
  3. Click anywhere once to switch the sound on (browsers require a click before sound).
  4. Plug the controllers into Windows; close other programs using them (Windows lets one program open a MIDI port). Hot-plug works (CT45 rescans).

Nothing comes from the internet: three.js r186, mp4-muxer 5.2.1 and the CT45 layer are in CT55.A1.MidiFlowers_lib/.

What you see

A flower emitter (a glowing pearl with a turning crown of pastel petals) floats 1.15 m above a pale paper-tiled floor. The view looks down on the floor (69° down, 15 m away) and follows the emitter as it travels right (+x); the emitter sits a little right of centre so the trail of flowers fills the left of the picture. Flowers (3 kinds: 5-petal blossom, 6-petal star, 12+12-petal daisy; bright pastel colours, yellow/cream centres, slightly self-lit so they read as bright) are thrown up out of the emitter, flutter down like petals (drag, sway, tilt, spin), land, slide a little and stay. All cast soft shadows (VSM shadow map, blurred), both in the air and on the floor.

Design notes

  • Flower state is structure-of-arrays (F.*), states 0 empty / 1 air / 2 landed / 3 swirling; three ring buffers (one per kind) recycle the oldest flower when 9000 are out. Air: gravity, drag 0.9/s up, 6.5/s down (≈1.5 m/s fall), sinusoidal sway + matching tilt. Swirl: velocity eased toward tangential ω·r with ω = 1.9·strength/(1+0.22 r), plus pull to each flower's own radius (0.7–4.5 m) and height (0.35–3.75 m), plus the travel velocity, centred on the emitter's track point.
  • Floor 640 m plane that jumps in whole 4 m tiles under the view (pattern stays put); shadow box and sun follow the view.
  • NeutralToneMapping, sRGB output; flowers get 20 % of their own colour as emission (bright pastel under shadow).
  • All actions go through CT55.act(name, phase); value controls through CT55.setValue(name, {delta}|{value}).
